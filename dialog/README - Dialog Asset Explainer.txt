How the Dialog Works — Plain English

The Basics

You walk up to someone in the game and click to talk. The game looks at a spreadsheet (the CSV file) to decide what they say.

Each row in the spreadsheet is one thing someone can say. It has:

    Who's talking
    Who they're talking to
    A unique name for that line
    What line has to come before it (if anything)
    Whether you need to know something first
    The actual words
    Whether you can say it again

Three Kinds of Lines

First lines — These start conversations. They don't need anything to come before them. Like "Evening Boss." When you first meet the barman, he says this.

Follow-ups — These only show up after something specific was just said. Like after the barman says "Where'd you dig up that janitor?", you can respond "Janitor? I didn't hire a janitor."

Unlocks — These appear when you've learned something somewhere else. Like after you learn the janitor's name is Vince, the barman suddenly remembers he's heard that name before.

How It Picks What to Say

The game looks for lines that:

    Match who you're talking to
    Either start fresh OR follow what was just said
    You have the right knowledge for (or don't need any)

Then it sorts them so lines you've never seen come first. If you've seen everything, it shows reusable lines (hints, greetings).

NPCs just say the first thing. You get to pick from the top 3-4 options.

Going Back and Forth

You can't get everything from one person in one visit. You have to:

    Meet the barman, learn about the janitor
    Go meet the janitor, learn his name
    Go back to the barman, who now remembers something
    Go to the DJ, who recognizes the name
    Back to the janitor, who starts remembering
    And so on

Each visit unlocks new branches. By the fourth visit to each person, you know the whole story.

Reusable Lines

Some things you can ask again and again — "How do I get to the basement?" "What was your name again?" These are marked "yes" in the reusable column.

Big story moments are marked "no" — they happen once, then never again.

The Short Version

You write lines in a spreadsheet. Link them with "previous" to make conversations. Add conditions so new stuff unlocks when you learn things. The game tracks what was said and shows you fresh options. NPCs pick the top one, you pick from the top few. Visit people multiple times to piece together the story.
